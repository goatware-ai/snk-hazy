Every coded rubric check tests the rubric against the golden; the fidelity axis, the reviewer
and adjudication test the golden against the documents it cites and against itself, and that is
where the fatal defects lived through nineteen rounds on hollenbach and a 0-error gate on
lift-truck-fleet-plan. Open before the rubric is drafted and again before every submission.

## 1. The standing read, before the rubric

Re-derive every attributed fact from the source that is supposed to carry it, then read the
golden against itself. The list, each item a check id or a hand read:

- **Every cited identifier exists in an input** (G10: invoice, PO, work-order ids); generators
  that renumber after the text was written are the usual cause. Every named PERSON comes from the
  inputs or the prompt (G15 owners, G16 authors and docProps creators, G17 titles): reviewers diff
  every surname (Villanueva, Spruill, Fontaine, Marcene Hildebrand as preparer), adjudication
  rules an unestablished name a fabricated identity, and the name sits in dc:creator and
  cp:lastModifiedBy too, so sweep the whole package. Use the prompt's requester or a
  memo-established person from the start, or leave the attribution unsigned.
- **A dated obligation on a named person carries a date from a document that mentions that
  person** (G11): "Balliet asked for it by Friday the 28th" when Balliet's message named no date
  and the 28th was another vendor's hold date is fabricated evidence, the most serious class.
- **Every full date the golden states appears in the prompt or an input or is derived by a
  stated rule** (G20, reading mm/dd/yy inputs, month-day, bare ordinals, ordinal weekdays, month
  ends, input date plus a named day count, and a plan table's BY WHEN column; G18 for dates on
  owner rows); never inferred from adjacent records (a return-to-service date). A date the golden
  invents as its own plan (a bid dated 30 April) goes in the actions table's date column, not in
  prose, and the prose points at "the last dated action in the table".
- **The deliverable is dated no later than any action it schedules as done** (G9), and a
  "ready to sign" ask means the signable document itself with a signature-and-date line (G8).
- **Every policy class applied to every record it governs, no invented status** ("runs under the
  card"), every entity carrying a per-vendor attribute shown with its test even where it does not
  bind (rempel: the Dettmann firm-price date appeared nowhere, R23 errors when a firm-price or
  validity date in an input appears in no solution cell), a "cost since <date>" covering the
  partial period, no duty on an asset after the day it leaves with the successor named, and a
  classifier ("the first step that missed its clock") run over the whole population with a NONE
  bucket so every record lands in a named outcome.
- **Counts, ranks and superlatives against the golden's own tables**: G5 lists every "X of the Y"
  prose claim for hand verification (ERROR when X > Y; "three of the four lost money" against a
  tab reading four was a fidelity 2/5); S1 re-verifies superlatives and ranks ("the cheapest of
  the four", "ranked last by value", "the last dated action") whenever numbers move, and after
  any edit list every rank word left by search; G19 checks a prose "moves up/down" against the
  correction column's sign; G7 asserts a docx table's total row equals its rows (an
  index-addressed python-docx edit hit the last data row and every figure check passed); G3
  requires a summary block enumerating the parts of a stated total to carry the reconciliation
  cell (5,117 stated against 5,159 enumerated survived three AutoEval rounds; the judge scores
  lines one at a time, a reviewer adds the column up); G1 fails a computed status (DE MINIMIS)
  beside a typed CHANGE token; G21 re-does the arithmetic on DISPLAYED figures as displayed (766
  x 7 is 5,362, not the printed 5,366), the fix always more decimals, never a recalculation.
- **Convention sentences and state words**: a convention stated in one section ("reported under
  every attribute it fails") is tested against every section it governs; every count word and
  every state word (open/closed/paid/flagged) is confirmed against the table or CSV column that
  carries it (review_check.py [fidelity] ledgers, BAR on a same-axis contradiction). Key each
  test on the column the policy sentence names, and re-key every date-gated test on every date
  column an input carries (PO_DATE vs APPROVED_AT moved a count from 11 to 12).
- **Parameter provenance** (G6): a Params literal cited to a docx section must appear in that
  section; when a generator constant changes, grep every input document for the old figure. A
  parameter row can cite the right clause and state the wrong basis ("ninety days before the
  freeze" where the policy says the review): when a policy names a date, grep every other place
  that policy's dates are set and confirm they name the same event.
- **Both directions against the source the inputs ship**: a golden that accepts an input figure
  because it "matched ours" has tested it against the generator's table; grep the golden for
  "matched" / "accepted" sentences and re-derive carrier and vendor figures from the shipped
  appendix (dfl: a fuel surcharge band half a point high on 43 of 122 bills).
- **Dispute answers name the entity the claimant named** (G14), or say why the ruling lands on
  another account.
- **A matching recomputation proves consistency, not correctness** (returns-cage, coded G22): a
  snapshot input stamped _MMDD must not reference record ids another input dates after the
  snapshot, and a docx balance "as of <date>" must not equal a register's sum only WITHOUT the
  date filter. Generator rule: pick the cutoff date FIRST and clamp every generated record date to
  it; records meant to postdate the story cutoff are excluded from every as-of figure and never
  referenced by a snapshot document.

## 2. The golden against the scenario

- A quotation's hold or expiry date binds the schedule: walk every action date against it and
  move the approval ahead of the plan where the timetable lands later. Vendor deadlines are not
  internal execution dates (scrap needs a physical-removal date). Every completed event is dated
  on or before the real submission date; a declared fictional "today" is not accepted.
- Net on-hand stock for every class of item the golden orders, on every line the inputs publish
  stock for (hollenbach ordered 538 with 163 on its own shelf). A group-average cost does not
  trace a SKU plan (597 unit-months against 897); item-level is proved by every item closing at
  zero and none negative. A blended cost never prices three supply sources at one unit cost
  (G13 ledgers one item carrying two unit costs). A plan is never built on an entitlement only
  REQUESTED (G12 ledgers the inputs' own conditions and prohibitions to tick off).
- Reviewers audit the gap between the triggering event and the data cut: what moved between
  them, who has to be called first, and what was not yet known when it moved; design tasks so
  that split exists and grade it.
- Where an input asks for copy addressed to a person (a posting, a script, a letter), the
  deliverable carries the words; transcribe the columns the ACTION needs (CONTACT, PHONE), not
  only the columns the sums need. New copy pays for itself in liveness when it reads THROUGH to
  the tabs behind it rather than restating figures (R59 twins).
- A physical-state phrase in a memo ("on the floor overnight") is a claim the log must show.
- A rule stated in one input plus an operand supplied by none (a substitute model's own net) is a
  required field with no source: list the operands of every prose formula and point at the cell
  or sentence supplying each; ground the value by adding the source page as an input rather than
  re-deriving it. Ask of every input table whether it publishes every parameter the golden
  computes with (the per-vendor freight rating appeared in no input).
- Decision-critical evidence (an executed amendment) must exist as a document in the inputs,
  not as a reference in correspondence. Data covers every window the sources cite (a 36-month
  rule needs 36 months of history); claimed quantities never exceed what the records document.
- "Pasted source" tabs stay verbatim (legend lines, row labels); weekday/date pairs are
  calendar-true in every file; derived calendar dates are re-run against their rule text ("thirty
  days after" compiled to day 29 was a send-back). A column header naming an input source is a
  provenance claim: a corrected figure under RECD PER REGISTER reads as unsupported even when
  right; rename the column to what the figure is, cite every clause that produces it, and check
  the cited clause actually triggers on the row.
- Avoid near-anagram item codes (DP-4102 / DP-7104 made a fidelity judge re-sum under the wrong
  code); give per-slot detail its own tab so the check happens inside the workbook, and scope
  every "falls in no measured window" note to its item.
- Close the golden with what it does not settle; write the prompt so it invites its own bad news;
  judgment, not row count, drives difficulty ([[review-task-workflow]] section 5).

## 3. Leakage and answer sets

- An approval or ruling document may rule on CATEGORIES and PROCEDURE; the moment it enumerates
  the chosen products it is the answer key (L1, ANSWER_LEAKED on one RFI answer). A memo RULE may
  set the test but never applies it to a named entity whose verdict a criterion scores (L3). Fix
  the input AND the golden cell that quotes it, at zip level. L2 covers hidden sheets, rows,
  comments, tracked changes and speaker notes; N1 project vocabulary; T1 the tell log outside the
  package ([[platform-expertdocs]]).
- An input may raise the concern but never name the members of a set a criterion scores as listed or
  flagged (L4, 2026-09-11): packaging-consolidation's Bay City email named kraft paper and peanuts as
  the two items dearer at the bidder and the adjudicator called it the answer to the exceptions row.
- The entity grounding check lists golden-only labels as fabricated (packaging-consolidation, 2026-09-11):
  bidder abbreviations the inputs never use (GLP, MPS), a column head in the golden's own words (STAYS
  LOCAL), and "PP-7 section 8" citations when the policy's parts are numbered headings with no
  "section" word. Label with the inputs' names, and cite a numbered part with its heading text
  ("section 8, What stays with the branches") so the string the check greps exists in the docx.
